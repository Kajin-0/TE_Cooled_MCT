Full thermal verification incomplete; not a commercially verified assembly.

Abstract. A steady, coupled thermoelectric finite-element calculation resolves 88 semiconductor legs,
interstage copper routing and a source-referenced two-ear, nine-pin header. The package drawing
and independently selected cooler datasheet define a synthetic composite; no evidence identifies this
as a manufactured assembly. At 0.1 A and 2 mW imposed die heating, the principal archived mesh
predicts a die mean of approximately 265.616 K and a terminal voltage of 0.551927 V. These are
conditional numerical outputs, not validated device performance.

On the initial fine mesh, current and weak energy checks pass, but independently integrated raw heat
flux does not meet all required limits. Neither a small algebraic residual nor a stable die temperature
resolves this qualification failure. The review draft is issued now to expose the corrected geometry,
calculations and limitations for technical review.

Latest refinement checkpoint. The cold-normal case passes at 4.795%, but the latest independent
bath error is 1.093% (FAIL). No combined passing case has been established. Numerical release
remains incomplete: a systematically refined comparator passing both gates is absent. The table
below retains earlier failures; subsequent solved results are documented separately.

Actual solid topology and discretization

Literature thermal model

For Hg0.71Cd0.29Te, the literature model [3] is represented by piecewise linear nodal conductivity:
(T, k) = (90, 2.41), (148, 1.61), (298, 0.973) and (400, 0.790) in kelvin and W m−1K−1. The calculation
stays within this range. Electrical conductivity of passive domains is a computational extension of
10−12 S m−1, not a physical detector or pin current prediction. Density and heat capacity do not
affect this steady calculation.

Interfaces have conformal shared nodes and ideal temperature/potential continuity. The explicit
joint and attach have finite bulk thermal resistance; no additional calibrated contact impedance is
added. Temperature-dependent, traceable P/N transport curves and measured contact resistances
have not been qualified for this hypothetical cooler. Earlier material studies cannot identify the
actual grade of an unspecified assembly.

3.1
Imposed conditions and exclusions

The electrical drive is 0.1 A with one hot-pad reference potential. Only the external Kovar underside
is held at 300 K. The die receives uniform volumetric heating totaling 2 mW: 1.5 mW imposed
bias dissipation plus 0.5 mW optical-like imposed heat. This does not simulate detector current,
absorption, responsivity or transmission through a window.

All other exposed solid surfaces are adiabatic; no arbitrary 300 K condition is imposed on the cold
assembly or pins. Copper supply and gold detector hybrid links are OFF. The source drawing’s
cap and window, any carrier, thermistor internals, gas, radiation and carrier-transport PDEs are
excluded. Their omission bounds the physical scope, even if numerical release is later achieved.

Figure 12: Independent three-dimensional, three-layer HeatSolver benchmark with an analytical
temperature field, harmonic transverse variation and a distributed 2 mW source. Source-face errors
are 12.5, 6.25 and 3.125%; mean-temperature errors are 1.855, 0.464 and 0.116 mK. The finest
benchmark passes its preregistered checks; this does not qualify the coupled device.

For a uniformly heated slab with constant conductivity, the axial solution includes a quadratic
term, T(z) = a + bz −qvz2/(2k). Its exact gradient varies linearly. A linear tetrahedron represents
temperature by an affine field and therefore has a constant gradient. Even when nodal temperatures
nearly match the analytical solution, the boundary trace samples a finite owner volume rather than
the exact boundary derivative. Normal refinement addresses that truncation directly.

The analytical mean temperature is 300.1958788 K. The finest distributed-source calculation gives
300.1957629 K. Source-face flux error halves at each level, while mean-temperature error decreases
by approximately four, consistent with first-order boundary gradients and second-order temperature
integration in this controlled benchmark. The actual coupled device has material jumps, geometric
edges and local refinement, so these rates are evidence for the mechanism, not transferable error
estimates.

9.1
Solver performance and current refinement studies

On the fixed fine mesh, the native direct-LU run completes in 1292.4 s with four nonlinear iterations
and about 5.71 GiB peak resident memory. Assembly costs 9.2–9.8 s per iteration; the linear solve
costs 257–343 s and dominates. A same-mesh replay using refinement with the current matrix and
numeric LU completes in 558.0 s, two iterations and 5.83 GiB. This warm replay is not a claim of
general cold-start speedup.

Subsequently completed normal-volume studies retain all physical equations, source terms and
acceptance limits. Their actual solved fields and current release status are documented in the
following section.

11
Ranked work needed before physical prediction

1. Establish both original raw limits on a production mesh and a systematically refined comparator.
Evaluate both owner-side contact powers and the raw external bath integral; continue normal
or transverse refinement as indicated by the actual residuals. Keep failed attempts and original
limits visible.

2. Quantify die temperature, terminal voltage and stage heat sensitivity. Establish asymptotic
behavior before claiming any whole-device GCI; a small change in one output is insufficient.

3. Identify the actual assembly, P/N grades, temperature-dependent transport data, solder/attach
thicknesses and contact impedances. Use independent calibration and holdout measurements.
Geometry agreement alone does not establish OEM validation.

4. Add and separately verify physical supply/detector links, cap/window, carrier and enclosure
heat transfer as needed. Pressure-dependent gas and surface radiation require new coupled
conservation and sensitivity checks; none is inherited from older assumed-geometry studies.

5. Compare identified device temperatures, electrical input and heat-rejection curves against
independent measurements with uncertainty. Resolve geometry/material uncertainty before
interpreting microkelvin-scale link effects or optical performance.

11.1
Reproducibility and review status

All figures in this edition are rebuilt from immutable corrected mesh and solved-field archives, with
audited node and tetrahedron correspondence. Exact plane intersections preserve geometric voids.
Machine-readable source inventory, checksums, execution records, figure inputs and build validation
accompany this edition separately from reader-facing pages. No solver is rerun merely to draw these
figures. The historical report is preserved as earlier assumed-geometry evidence; its figures are not
used here.

This document is available for technical review now. Full numerical qualification and physical
assembly validation remain incomplete. Numerical precision in tables is for reproducibility; unknown
materials, contacts and omitted paths prevent a defensible device-performance uncertainty interval.
