Hypothetical reference article. Numerical release incomplete.

Abstract. A source-referenced nine-pin, two-ear housing and an independently selected three-
stage cooler envelope define a synthetic HgCdTe detector article. This assessment combines two
distinct numerical descriptions. A genuine coupled three-dimensional solid calculation resolves
88 thermoelectric legs, internal conductors, a header, explicit joint and attach layers, and the
detector. At 0.1 A, 300 K underside bath and 2 mW imposed die heat, its principal fine mesh gives
approximately 265.6 K die temperature and 0.552 V. Current and conservative heat checks pass, while
original raw boundary-gradient requirements remain unmet: the principal ceramic/attach mismatch
is 6.48% against 5%, and the best independent normal-refinement bath error is 1.069% against 1%.
Later transverse cases remain near 1.29%. No passing combined mesh and systematically refined
comparator have established numerical release.

A separate five-temperature model uses bounded nonlinear one-dimensional P/N transport, corrected
wire routes, a configurable sink, imposed detector heating and preliminary gas/radiation exchange.
Its enclosure is an explicitly filled stepped-core proxy inside candidate cap/window solids, not a
fully resolved native cavity. At nominal 300 K, 0.15 A, 30 kPa krypton and 2 mW die heat, this
model predicts approximately 232.2 K and 1.152 V. Of 63 prescribed scenario/current points, 52
are accepted numerically and 11 remain rejected with diagnostics. Material laws above 300 K
are assumed; no optical transmission, detector carrier transport or measured device-performance
uncertainty is established.

Engineering interpretation. Source geometry, physical hypotheses, numerical verification and
empirical accuracy are treated separately. Numerical digits identify conditional calculations, not
manufacturing tolerances. Neither the VIGO housing plus candidate cooler reference nor the reduced
enclosure model is a verified commercial assembly.
The report supports bounded engineering
exploration; it does not certify a device or a numerical release.

1.2
Meaning of verification

A converged algebraic solve, current conservation, an accurate controlled benchmark and empirical
device validation answer different questions. The first three can support a numerical implementation
without identifying real materials or package construction. Raw-gradient qualification is stricter than
a conservative reaction balance. Chosen low/nominal/high scenarios are engineering alternatives,
not confidence intervals. The sections below keep these distinctions visible for every result.

Actual solid topology and discretization

the terminal drive acts directly on the resolved module pad faces. The nine pins remain passive
thermal solids with exposed ends adiabatic.

Literature thermal model

For Hg0.71Cd0.29Te, the literature model [3] is represented by piecewise linear nodal conductivity:
(T, k) = (90, 2.41), (148, 1.61), (298, 0.973) and (400, 0.790) in kelvin and W m−1K−1. The calculation
stays within this range. Electrical conductivity of passive domains is a computational extension of
10−12 S m−1, not a physical detector or pin current prediction. Density and heat capacity do not
affect this steady calculation.

Interfaces have conformal shared nodes and ideal temperature/potential continuity. The explicit
joint and attach have finite bulk thermal resistance; no additional calibrated contact impedance is
added. Temperature-dependent, traceable P/N transport curves and measured contact resistances
have not been qualified for this hypothetical cooler. Earlier material studies cannot identify the
actual grade of an unspecified assembly.

4.1
Imposed conditions and exclusions

The electrical drive is 0.1 A with one hot-pad reference potential. Only the external Kovar underside
is held at 300 K. The die receives uniform volumetric heating totaling 2 mW: 1.5 mW imposed
bias dissipation plus 0.5 mW optical-like imposed heat. This does not simulate detector current,
absorption, responsivity or transmission through a window.

All other exposed solid surfaces are adiabatic; no arbitrary 300 K condition is imposed on the cold
assembly or pins. Copper supply and gold detector hybrid links are OFF. The source drawing’s
cap and window, any carrier, thermistor internals, gas, radiation and carrier-transport PDEs are
excluded. These exclusions apply to this solid-only field. The separate reduced package model later
includes specified wire and enclosure exchanges without creating a three-dimensional cavity field.

Resistivity ρ is in Ωm, conductivity is identically σ = 1/ρ, thermopower S is in V K−1, and k is in

All evaluated states must be finite, with positive ρ and k and the correct P/N sign of S. No solution
is accepted by clipping it to a temperature limit. Interior Joule heating can activate the assumed
continuation even when both boundary temperatures are below 300 K. Continuation tests establish
internal consistency only, not experimental validation. The research-specimen choice, digitization
and assumed extension are consequential physical uncertainties.

Figure 13: Independent three-dimensional, three-layer HeatSolver benchmark with an analytical
temperature field, harmonic transverse variation and a distributed 2 mW source. Source-face errors
are 12.5, 6.25 and 3.125%; mean-temperature errors are 1.855, 0.464 and 0.116 mK. The finest
benchmark passes its benchmark-specific preregistered checks; these do not replace the production
bath criterion or qualify the coupled device.

For a uniformly heated slab with constant conductivity, the axial solution includes a quadratic
term, T(z) = a + bz −qvz2/(2k). Its exact gradient varies linearly. A linear tetrahedron represents
temperature by an affine field and therefore has a constant gradient. Even when nodal temperatures
nearly match the analytical solution, the boundary trace samples a finite owner volume rather than
the exact boundary derivative. Normal refinement addresses that truncation directly.

The analytical mean temperature is 300.1958788 K. The finest distributed-source calculation gives
300.1957629 K. Source-face flux error halves at each level, while mean-temperature error decreases
by approximately four, consistent with first-order boundary gradients and second-order temperature
integration in this controlled benchmark. The actual coupled device has material jumps, geometric
edges and local refinement, so these rates are evidence for the mechanism, not transferable error
estimates.

11.2
Computational method and practical cost

The fixed fine solid problem was solved with a native direct factorization and checked with a same-
mesh numeric-refinement replay. The principal native calculation takes approximately 1,292 s and
5.7 GiB peak resident memory; a warm replay completes in approximately 558 s and 5.8 GiB. This is
a warm-start comparison, not a general cold-start speed guarantee. The later coupled transverse
fields complete in approximately 911 and 999 s with three nonlinear iterations. These numerical
costs motivate bounded transport/exchange reductions for engineering scenarios, without implying
that the reduced model reproduces the full spatial boundary-value problem.

14.2
Meaning of apparent numerical precision

Several tables retain extra digits to distinguish solved states and audit balances. Without identified

digits do not provide a physical accuracy interval. A microkelvin-scale change in an output can
coexist with a failed raw spatial flux criterion, as the solid refinements demonstrate. A small
reduced-model residual similarly cannot certify a real detector temperature.

17.1
Engineering conclusions

The source-referenced solid model demonstrates a two-terminal 88-leg coupled calculation with
meaningful electrical/heat verification, while exposing unresolved raw boundary gradients. Stable
temperature and voltage do not remove the original release failures. The direct nonlinear transport
option markedly improves high-current consistency with a refined one-dimensional reference at
practical scenario cost. Its physical grade and high-temperature continuation remain assumptions.

The preliminary enclosure operator makes reservoir heat exchange explicit and conservative, and
records its geometric corrections. Its filled-core closure is useful for bounded comparisons, but it
should not be interpreted as an accurate hermetic-cavity field. Native interleg/pin surfaces, contact
and bypass paths, and cap reservoir behavior are the important unresolved physical descriptions. A
large geometry refinement alone cannot establish their correctness.

For the specified nominal reduced scenario, approximately 232.2 K die temperature, 0.173 W electrical
input and 0.182 W sink rejection describe one conditional synthetic operating point. Material-law
and imposed-load sensitivity dominate the chosen scenario spans. Neither an optimum current nor a
defensible device-performance confidence interval is established. The assessment supports transparent
engineering exploration while maintaining a clear boundary between computation, hypothesis and
measurement.

17.2
Reproducible scientific method

The three-dimensional figures use exact intersections of stored tetrahedra and solved fields, with
independent node/cell correspondence; voids are retained. The reduced figures are calculated from
completed scenario data and independent boundary-value comparisons, with failures visible. Native
candidate geometry is audited separately from the proxy. Sources, numerical inputs, archived field
identities and deterministic figure/build records are retained outside the reader-facing pages. No
field is invented to draw a figure and no additional production solve is implied by a figure rebuild.
