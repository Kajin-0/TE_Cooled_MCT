{\Large Three-Dimensional Engineering Assessment\par}
\vspace{5mm}
Revision 2\hfill 29 October 2026
\par\rule{\linewidth}{.5pt}
\textbf{Fine-mesh qualification: not qualified.}
Three converged fine meshes fail the unchanged independent external-bath
raw-gradient heat-flux criterion of $1\%$, with errors of $1.51$--$1.66\%$.
Coarse numerical passes and conservative reaction balance do not override this failure.

\textbf{Abstract.}
A steady coupled finite-element model evaluates a three-stage thermoelectric
cooler and HgCdTe detector within a nine-pin TO-66 enclosure. The construction
contains 215 solid domains and all 88 thermoelectric legs. Actual tetrahedral
boundaries and solved temperature and electric-potential fields support every
geometry and field illustration. Gas heat transfer is a pressure-dependent
surface-film reduction; diffuse-grey radiation and axial wire/lead operators
are coupled to the solid solution. Ten coarse operating cases satisfy their
per-case numerical checks. The illustrated nitrogen condition uses
\NodeCount\ nodes, \TetCount\ tetrahedra and 907 exchange patches, with a
300 K external header bath, 0.1 A drive and 2 mW detector deposition at 100 Pa.
Its die volume mean is \FieldDie\ K, a result for declared hypotheses rather
than a manufacturer-accurate or measured detector temperature.

Pre-solder at the header/alumina interface is physically present according to
the supplied hardware information. The model uses a generic 50 $\mu$m,
$k=2$ W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ mount proxy; it is not a metallurgical pre-solder model.
Material-grade identification, measured interfaces, spectral window transport,
carrier transport and device validation remain incomplete.

\section{Model geometry}
The solved construction combines a three-stage, 44-couple TEC with a Kovar-like
header, finite-volume mount and die attach, HgCdTe die, Au pads, nine glass/pin
pairs and an attached cap/window. A unique conductor path has 181 vertices,
180 edges and two external terminals. The 28/12/4 stage allocation, buried
routes and ceramic supports are inferred construction, not verified manufacturer
artwork. The package family envelope is supported by the public drawing [\ref{ref:drawing}].

pins, cap and window temperatures are unknowns. External cap/window surfaces
and isolated pin ends are adiabatic. Cap/header and window/rim contacts impose
ideal thermal continuity, without identified seal resistance.

\section{Governing physics}
\subsection{Coupled solids and boundary conditions}
Elmer's coupled thermoelectric solver determines spatial temperature $T$ and
potential $\phi$ throughout the solid mesh. The sign convention is
\begin{align}
\mathbf E&=-\nabla\phi,&\mathbf j&=\sigma_e(\mathbf E-S\nabla T),&
\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf j&=0,\\
\mathbf q&=-k\nabla T+ST\mathbf j,&
\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf q&=\mathbf j\!\cdot\mathbf E+q'''_{\rm det}.
\end{align}
$\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf E$ is electrical work density; irreversible Joule
heating is $|\mathbf j|^2/\sigma_e$. Ideal conformal contacts retain temperature
and potential continuity. The TEC includes Seebeck, Peltier and Joule coupling.
Its constant mixed verification law is not an identified manufacturer
$S(T),\sigma_e(T),k(T)$ dataset.

The 2 mW detector deposition is one bias/optical thermal representation.
A second detector Joule term is not added. The model does not solve carrier
transport, bias-dependent absorption or thermistor electrical behavior.

Package solids use $S=0$ and a tiny computational conductivity,
$\sigma_e=10^{-12}$ S m$^{-1}$. Their potential is a passive numerical extension,
not real Kovar/Au electrical transport. Electrical figures therefore interpret
P/N/Cu only. Spurious package Joule heat stays below 1 nW, and a separate
100-fold conductivity reduction changes the die mean by less than
$5\times10^{-7}$ K in the tested coarse case.

\subsection{Hybrid wire and lead paths}
Each axial connection has $G=kA/L$. Au bonds use $k=315$ W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$
and equal/opposite thermal powers. Cu leads use $k=400$ W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ and
$\rho_e=1.72\times10^{-8}$ $\Omega$ m, with $R=\rho_e L/A$.
For a lead connecting two solved endpoint temperatures, the two inward powers are
\begin{equation}
P_1=G(T_2-T_1)+\tfrac12 I^2R,\qquad
P_2=G(T_1-T_2)+\tfrac12 I^2R.
\end{equation}
Thus axial conduction cancels in the whole-package budget; the lead electrical
work remains. These conservative operators update during the nonlinear solid
solve. They are a 3D-solid/1D-wire hybrid, not an all-3D wire model.

\subsection{Surface radiation and pressure-dependent gas paths}
Diffuse-grey radiosity is coupled nonlocally to the current surface temperatures:
\begin{align}
J_i^{\rm rad}&=\epsilon_i\sigma_{\rm SB}T_i^4+
 (1-\epsilon_i)\sum_jF_{ij}J_j^{\rm rad},\\
P_i^{\rm rad,in}&=-A_i\left(J_i^{\rm rad}-\sum_jF_{ij}J_j^{\rm rad}\right),\\
\sum_jF_{ij}&=1,& A_iF_{ij}&=A_jF_{ji}.
\end{align}
Deterministic first-hit rays account for occlusion. Symmetric area balancing
preserves blocked-pair zeros. Balanced closure below $10^{-10}$ and reciprocal
area error below $10^{-15}$ m$^2$ establish conservation, not exact view factors.
For 907 patches, raw reciprocity L1/area is 0.213 and raw area-weighted closure
error is 0.0136. Finite-ray sampling, patch resolution and hit mapping limit accuracy.

The reduced gas-film network uses symmetric exchange area $W_{ij}=A_iF_{ij}$
and harmonic normal-projected first-hit flight gap $L_{ij}$:
\begin{align}
G_{ij}&=\frac{k_g(\overline T_{ij})W_{ij}}{L_{ij}+2\ell_T},&
P_i^{\rm gas,in}&=\sum_jG_{ij}(T_j-T_i),\\
\lambda&=\frac{\mu}{p}\sqrt{\frac{\pi R_gT}{2}},&
\ell_T&=\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\frac{\lambda}{\mathrm{Pr}},\\
\mathrm{Kn}&=\lambda/L,&\mu&=\frac{k_g}{C_p+1.25R_g}.
\end{align}
Here $R_g$ is the specific gas constant and $\alpha=0.8$ is an accommodation
hypothesis. Eucken viscosity and constant ideal-gas $C_p,\gamma$ are closures.
The implementation gives exactly zero gas exchange at $p=0$ and recovers
parallel-plane continuum and Kennard free-molecular limits [\ref{ref:gap}].
This is a reduced thermal path, not a gas-volume conduction PDE or CFD solution.

The thermal window is internally opaque grey ($\tau=0$) and externally adiabatic.
Spectral LWIR transmission, optical coatings and ambient radiance are absent.
Prescribed absorbed optical heat does not predict transmission through the window.

\textbf{Pre-solder is physically present at the header/alumina interface.}
The 50 $\mu$m, $k=2$ W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ proxy gives
$R''=t/k=25\times10^{-6}$ m$^2$ K W$^{-1}$ before spreading. It does not identify
an alloy, wetting layer, intermetallic structure or measured joint resistance.
Replacing it requires new physical inputs and new verification.

The MCT source reports conductivity uncertainty of approximately 7--14\%; this
is not a package-temperature confidence interval. No composition interpolation,
material stitching or out-of-domain extrapolation is used. The gas source gives
generally below 3\% conductivity uncertainty, which also does not include the
surface-film reduction or package construction uncertainty.

Geometry, material grade, interfaces, enclosure heat paths and manufacturer
calibration remain confounded. Constant coefficients, opaque window assumptions
and steady heat-capacity placeholders do not qualify low-temperature material
curves, optical responsivity or transients. Display precision is numerical
precision, not an empirical temperature uncertainty.

\section{Recommendations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Resolve the independent header-bath gradient failure using systematic
boundary/interface refinement or an independently verified discretization.
Retain the 1\% gate and report reaction and gradient diagnostics separately.
\item Identify the actual header/alumina pre-solder, die attach and seal
construction, including alloy, thickness, contact area and thermal resistance.
Replace generic proxies only with new, separately verified cases.
\item Obtain matched-grade, temperature-dependent P/N transport and independent
loaded device measurements. Reserve calibration and holdout conditions before
fitting; require matched bath, drive, detector load and environment.
\item Characterize the real feedthrough arrangement, wire lengths and optical
window. Treat spectral transmission, external radiance and distributed wire
gas/radiation exchange as additional physics requiring verification.
\item Compare electrical power at a common detector target after numerical and
device qualification. Fixed-current gas comparisons alone do not establish a
production gas choice, optimum cooling performance or reliability.
\end{enumerate}
All figures here derive from existing solved fields. This assessment revises
presentation only; it introduces no new physical execution or acceptance claim.
